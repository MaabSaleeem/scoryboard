Your profile is public. Anybody signed in to Scoryboard can open it, read your statistics and follow you. Scoryboard has no preview of your own profile, so the way to check what visitors see is to open another player's.

Pick a player who plays the same sport as you. There are two layouts, football and padel, and they are not the same page. The padel one is at the foot of this article.

See your profile the way others do

  1. Open any player's profile: search for them, or select their name anywhere it appears. What you see there is what other people see when they open yours. {{shot:01|A player profile seen by somebody else: banner, photo, name, rating and counters, with the Follow button outlined.}}
  2. Read the controls across the top of the banner. A visitor gets Back, Chat, Request Payment and Add To Team. {{shot:02|The controls across the top of the banner: Back on the left, then Chat, Request Payment and Add To Team.}}
  3. Read what is public: your bio, your ten statistic tiles, your team rank and the teams you are in. {{shot:03|My bio, the ten statistic tiles, Team rank and Teams, as a visitor sees them.}}
  4. Read the Matches panel. A visitor can switch between Past Matches and Upcoming Matches, but has no Create Match button. That one is only on your own home page. {{shot:04|The Matches panel on Past Matches, showing one finished match and no Create Match button.}}
  5. Select Follow to see that player's activity. The button then reads Unfollow.

What you will see

Your email address and your date of birth are not on your profile. They stay on Profile settings, where only you can read them.

The Views counter is on your own home page and not on the copy other people see.

A padel profile is a different page

A padel player's profile is not the football page with padel numbers in it. Four things are different, and a football reader who opens one will not recognise it.

  1. Open a padel player's profile. A switch at the top right holds two pills, Football and Padel. Which one it opens on is that player's own choice, not yours. {{shot:05|A padel player's profile seen by a visitor. The two-pill Football and Padel switch at the top right is outlined.}}
  2. Read the tiles. Five of the ten are the same as a football profile's - matches, wins, losses, draws and W/L ratio. The other five are padel's own: WIN RATE, BEST HAND, COURT POSITION, MATCH TYPE and PREFERRED TIME. There are no goals, no assists and no cards. {{shot:06|The ten tiles on a padel profile: Matches, Win, Loss, Draw and W/L Ratio above Win rate, Best hand, Court position, Match type and Preferred time.}}

The other two differences are things that are not there. A padel profile has no Matches panel, so a visitor cannot read a padel player's match history from it. It has no Leaderboards table; a PADEL TOURNAMENTS panel stands at the foot of the page instead.

It also gains a LEVEL panel, which is that player's padel rating. See "Your teams, rankings and match history" for the same page as its owner sees it.

You can land here without meaning to. A profile opens on whichever side its owner set as their default, so a football-only reader who follows step 1 and picks a padel player gets this page rather than the one above.

If it does not work

  • Your statistics are empty to visitors. They are empty to you too. Statistics come from finished matches.
  • Your photo or bio is missing. A visitor sees whatever you have saved. Set them on Profile settings.
  • You cannot find a preview of your own profile. There is not one. Open another player's profile instead - one who plays your sport, because football and padel profiles are laid out differently.
  • You cannot find your own profile link. Search your own name in the sidebar and select yourself.
