## The finding that shapes everything below

**Chat has a full REST surface, and Free cannot read it.**

Two things were unknown when this collection started, and both were settled on
2026-09-02 against staging. They are written up in `config/api.md`, "Chat and
messaging"; this is the short version, because every decision further down follows
from them.

1. **Chat is ordinary REST.** `config/personas.yaml` said to seed the group chat
   through the UI, because the Postman collection holds only the admin chat-report
   resolver. Reading the app bundle turned up nineteen `/chats` endpoints, all
   bearer-token REST, and `scripts/seed-18.mjs` now builds the whole transcript
   over them. Firestore carries the live read; the Realtime Database carries
   presence. Both files have been corrected.

2. **On Free, an incoming message is ten characters.** `GET .../messages` returns
   somebody else's message as `"I am in. I..."` with `isContentLocked: true`.
   Replying to one is `403 {"reason":"Upgrade to Pro to access the full message
   content"}`, and so is reacting to one. In the app every incoming bubble carries
   an **Unlock with Pro** button instead of its text.

   `config/api.md` described `CHAT_PRO_REQUIRED` as "reading an incoming message in
   full". That is the read half. The write half - reply and react refused outright -
   was not recorded anywhere, and it is what makes 18.3 a Pro article.

**`targetUid` and `memberUids` are Firebase uids, not playerIds.** Read `uid` off
`GET /users/me`. `lib/api.mjs`'s `mintSession` does not return one - its `localId`
is undefined against this Identity Toolkit response - so every caller here goes
through `/users/me`.

Re-seed with `node scripts/seed-18.mjs`. It is idempotent for the accounts, teams,
leaderboard, squad and friends; it **deletes and rebuilds every conversation** on
each run, on purpose. A transcript is append-only, so a half-failed run would leave
messages the next run appends to. No spec may hardcode a conversation id or a
message id: they change every seed. The specs look both up by text.

## Changed during the run

`docs/workflow.md`: changing the brief mid-run is allowed; changing it quietly is
not. Seven things moved between writing this and executing it. None of them
changes what an article says - they are all about how a capture is taken.

1. **18.1 shot 02 is one group row, not the Chats column.** The column is an
   `<aside>` 899 pixels tall with two rows at the top and white space for the rest,
   so the first version published a strip that was nine tenths empty. The row
   carries everything the shot was for: avatar, title, the `Group` tag, the last
   message and its time. `conversationRow()` in `lib/kb.ts`.

2. **Transcript timestamps are not masked.** The brief said to mask them and the
   style guide lists "absolute dates and times that are not the point". A collection
   18 capture has twelve to fourteen of them - one under every bubble, one on every
   conversation row - and masking all of them turns the transcript into a column of
   black rectangles, which is worse for the reader than the thing being hidden. They
   are `h:mm` on the day the seed ran, with no date beside them, and they identify
   nobody. The clinching detail is Open questions 4: the app renders an edited
   message's footer as one node, `8:34 · edited`, so a regex loose enough to catch
   the time paints over the word 18.3's last capture exists to show. The long
   version of this decision is in `lib/kb.ts` where `chat18Times()` used to be.
   **What is still hidden:** the signed-in name, and all three unread badges.

3. **18.3 captures out of order.** A bubble whose menu has been opened keeps its
   chevron for good - not while hovered, and not while focused. Parking the pointer
   did not clear it and neither did blurring the trigger, and two runs went out with
   a chevron on the two bubbles that had been opened and none on the rest. So the
   three at-rest captures (03, 05, 09) are taken first, before any menu exists. The
   filenames still carry the article's order.

4. **18.3 shot 09 is the whole conversation column.** It was meant to be a padded
   crop around the deleted message that also reached the edited one. It cannot be:
   your own messages are right-aligned and everybody else's are left-aligned, so the
   two are at opposite edges of an 800-pixel column. The first version published a
   frame with the edited message nowhere in it.

5. **18.1 shots 01 and 05 have no annotation.** 01's subject is the whole
   three-column page, and an outline around a full-height column is two red lines
   running off the top and bottom edges. 05's subject is the pattern - every incoming
   message is a stub - and an outline around one of six identical **Unlock with Pro**
   buttons says the opposite. 05 also hit the mechanical problem in 6.

6. **Inside the transcript, annotate only through a clipped capture.** The
   transcript scrolls in its own container, so `window.scrollY` stays 0 and
   `annotate()` cannot convert a viewport box into a document box. An outline drawn
   on a full-page capture lands below its target; 18.1 shot 05 first came back with
   one around an empty patch of background. `shot()`'s clipped branch scrolls first
   and draws afterwards, which is the order that works.

7. **Nothing is submitted in 18.3 at all.** The brief allowed one exception - toggle
   a reaction on, then off. It is not needed: the seed already leaves a 👍 on Milo's
   message, so the picker is opened and photographed and never chosen from.

Two more things were found while executing, and both are now in `config/api.md`
rather than only here:

- **Deleting a message is not confirmed.** Both *Delete for me* and *Delete for
  everyone* fire on the click. Found by choosing *Delete for everyone* on a seeded
  message during exploration, which vanished; the seed was re-run to put it back.
  18.3 therefore photographs the menu and never the click.
- **The transcript paginates on scroll** and shows a "Loading older messages..."
  pill while it does - a plain text pill, not an `.animate-pulse`, so `shot()`'s
  skeleton backstop does not catch it. One run of 18.3 shot 09 published one, on a
  group with no older messages to load. `transcriptSettled()` now gates on it.

## Open questions

1. **Announcement only is never switched on.** It is photographed in Group Info and
   described in prose, but not exercised. Switching it silences the group for
   everybody except its one admin, and there is no second admin to switch it back
   without a `PATCH .../group/admins/:uid {"action":"grant"}` the article does not
   document. Assumed: describing it is enough for 18.2. If the reviewer wants the
   silenced state shown, it needs a second seeded group.
2. **Attachments are not documented.** The composer has a paperclip,
   `POST /chats/media` exists and up to five files may ride on one message. The
   owner cut "sending messages, photos and files" on 2026-08-29, so nothing here
   covers it, and `attachmentsLocked` means Free cannot open one anyway. Assumed:
   the cut was deliberate. Flag if a reader asks how to send a photo.
3. **There are no read receipts.** The cut article "read receipts" has nowhere to
   go. `memberState[uid].lastReadAt` is the only trace and nothing renders it; the
   single tick beside an outgoing message is drawn unconditionally and does not
   change when the other person reads it. Assumed: no article, and 18.1 does not
   mention ticks. Worth a ticket if the tick is meant to mean something.
4. **The `edited` tag shares a node with its timestamp.** The transcript renders
   `8:21 · edited` as one element, so a mask over times would cover the word 18.3
   shot 09 is about. The specs mask exact `h:mm` nodes only, which leaves that one
   timestamp legible. Assumed: one visible time in two captures is better than
   hiding the subject.
5. **Presence dots move.** The green/red dot on an avatar comes from the Realtime
   Database and reflects who is signed in at that moment. The specs run one context
   at a time so the dots are consistent within a run, but they are not stable across
   runs if anything else is signed in to these accounts. Assumed: harmless. It is
   the likeliest source of a between-run difference in this collection.
