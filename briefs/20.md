## Read this first

**Three of the four mapped titles promise a control this build does not have,
and all three are retitled below rather than dropped.** Everything here was
measured on staging on 2026-09-03, endpoint by endpoint and screen by screen.
Nothing is invented and nothing is quietly narrowed.

1. **There is no notification filter.** Notifications are a modal opened from
   the bell, not a route - `/notifications` answers **404 from the server**, and
   so do `/home` and `/activities`. The modal's own hook only ever sends
   `{limit: 10, skip: n}`, read out of the bundle and confirmed on the wire, and
   the modal carries exactly three controls: **Mark all as read** in its header,
   and per row a **Mark as read** tick and a trash button. No chips, no
   dropdown, no tabs, nothing keyed to a type or to the read state.

   `GET /notifications` *does* accept `type` and `isRead`. Nothing in the app
   sends either, and **`isRead` does not work**: `?isRead=false`, `true`, `0`,
   `1` and `False` all answer an empty list on an account holding sixteen unread
   rows. So 20.1's "filtering" is dropped from the title.

2. **There is no activity-feed filter either, and the feed is called
   Trending.** `GET /activities` takes `referenceType`, `teamId`,
   `leaderboardId` and `tournamentId`. The component that renders the feed takes
   a `query` prop that becomes those parameters - and **no caller passes one.**
   Swept across 65 chunks pulled from every route in the app's own `Routes`
   enum, the component is used twice and both times as
   `<Trending containerClassName="rounded-lg border bg-white p-3" />`: once in
   the football profile layout, once in the padel one. Same panel, no query, no
   title override, no `emptyMessage` override.

   So 20.4's "how to filter it" is dropped, and the article is named after what
   the panel is actually called, as `docs/style-guide.md` requires of every
   product noun.

3. **"Every email Scoryboard sends you" is a promise this collection cannot
   keep.** Fifteen distinct subjects were collected out of the four accounts'
   inboxes after driving every flow collection 20 can reach, and they are 20.2's
   table. Two groups are missing: the **payment** emails
   (`PaymentRequested`, `PaymentReceived`, `PaymentFailed` are in the API's own
   enum and all three need a connected Stripe payout account, which
   `briefs/17.md` records as un-rebuildable from here) and the
   **tournament-organiser** emails (`TournamentUpdate` needs a tournament phase
   ended with the "notify followers" option, which is collections 13 and 15).
   An article called "Every email" would have to list those or be wrong. 20.2 is
   retitled and lists what was observed.

Everything in 20.3 matched the map.

## Two defects that shape the whole run

**The badge is a running tally in Firestore, and it is not clamped.**
`GET /notifications` answers a bare array with no counters. The bell badge and
the modal title read `{totalCount, unreadCount, notificationIds}` off an
`onSnapshot` listener on the Firestore document `notifications/<firebase uid>`.
That number is arithmetic, not a count:

- `PATCH /notifications/mark-all-read` **sets** `unreadCount` to 0;
- `PATCH /notifications/mark-read {ids, isRead: false}` puts every row back to
  unread and **leaves the tally alone** - measured at list-unread 16 with the
  badge absent and the modal titled plain "Notifications";
- `DELETE /notifications/:id` on an unread row **subtracts one**, so clearing a
  list whose tally is already 0 drives it negative. Pia's read
  `{totalCount: 11, unreadCount: -21}` after one exploratory pass, and a
  negative tally renders no badge and no Mark all as read link, because both are
  gated on `unreadCount > 0`.

20.1 photographs the badge and it photographs Mark all as read, so **the seed
must run before every capture** and it must heal the tally: mark everything read
*before* deleting it, which sets the tally to 0 rather than driving it negative,
then let the eleven new notifications take it to eleven. `scripts/seed-20.mjs`
reads the Firestore document back at the end and refuses to finish unless it
says 11.

**The Trending strip scrolls itself every 2.5 seconds.** A `setTimeout` in the
carousel hook calls `scrollTo` on the list, advances its own `activeIndex` and
reschedules - and it keeps doing that under a frozen clock, because
`page.clock.setFixedTime` pins `Date.now()` and leaves timers running.
Measured: scrollLeft went 0, 399, 797, 1196 over three intervals.
`stopTrendingAutoAdvance()` drops timers asked for at exactly that delay - 2500
appears **once** in the whole bundle - and `parkTrending20()` asserts both that
the stub is in the page and that the strip is on its first card.

**Reconciled**: the four accounts, their memberships, the venue and the

comments and every one of Pia's notifications. Three reasons, all API facts:

- the badge tally only goes up when a notification is made (see "Two defects");
- a comment cannot be deleted at all (`briefs/19.md`), so an exact thread needs
  a new entity to hang off;
- a **team** follow answers `409 Already following` a second time and an
  invitation cannot be accepted twice, so those generators fire once per entity.

Team ids, the leaderboard id, the match id, every comment id and every
notification id therefore change on every run. `lib/kb.ts`'s `fixtures20()`
looks each one up by name and throws with the seed command when it is missing.

### The match date is FIXED, and in the future

`2026-12-01T19:00:00.000Z`. Two reasons, and neither is a preference.

**`MatchLive` fires only from an explicit `POST /matches/:id/status
{"status":"Live"}`.** A match whose date has passed starts itself - collection
10's finding - and the automatic start notifies nobody, so a past-dated match
yields MatchInvitation and MatchSummary and never MatchLive. A Scheduled match
can be walked Scheduled -> Live -> Finished by hand and all three arrive.

**And all three print the date into their own sentence** - "Match scheduled KB
Notify FC X KB Notify Rovers **on 01 Dec 2026, 07:00 PM** at KB Notify Astro". A
relative date would move that phrase every run, in the middle of a row whose
text is not what the article is about, and masking a phrase inside three rows
reads as damage.

`scripts/seed-20.mjs` **refuses to run once that date has passed**, the way
`scripts/seed-09.mjs` and `scripts/seed-10.mjs` do with theirs. Move it, and
re-capture 20.1.

The rendered date depends on the context timezone. December is GMT in
`Europe/London`, so 19:00 UTC is 07:00 PM there; 20.1 asserts the exact phrase
so a spec run on another timezone fails rather than publishing a different one.

shuffled. `scripts/seed-20.mjs` now waits for each row to be Pia's newest before
firing the next, which is a condition and not a sleep.

### The four wording defects the captures carry

Visible in 20.1's shots and **not mentioned in any article's prose.**
`briefs/19.md` settled that rule after the repo owner had a line about a
mislabelled picker cut from 19.3: a defect belongs in the brief, the report and
a ticket, not in an article that teaches a reader to work around it.

1. `CommentLike` reads "**Liked your comment comment on**" - the word twice, and
   a capital L mid-sentence.
2. `LeaderboardComment` reads "Ollie **commented** KB Notify League" - no "on".
3. The `TeamFollow` row renders "following KB Pia United **.**" with a space
   before the full stop - the team name is its own button and the stop is a
   separate span after it.
4. The `TeamFollow` email subject reads "started following your team
   **onScoryBoard!**" - no space.

A fifth, from the API rather than the UI: **`PaymentFailed` is in the
notification type enum and the app's renderer has no case for it**, so it falls
through to the default, "You have a new notification."

### 20.1 Your notifications - reading, marking and deleting

- Persona: **player** (`kb-player-20@`, Free). Shot 06 is taken as **empty**
  (`kb-20-empty@`), who holds none.
- Preconditions found:
  - **Notifications are a modal, not a route.** `/notifications` answers 404
    from the server. The bell that opens it is in the **sidebar identity row**,
    beside the reader's own name.
  - **There are two bells and only one is on screen at desktop width.** The
    header carries a `lucide-bell` marked `md:hidden`, which is in the DOM with
    a zero-sized box at 1440px and is what a class-based locator finds first -
    and then cannot click. The sidebar's is a bare inline `<svg>` with no lucide
    class, found by its own path data.
  - **Ten rows to a page**, fetched `{limit: 10, skip: n}` and appended on
    scroll. The handler fires when the scroll reaches the bottom; a
    programmatic `scrollTop` write does not fire React's `onScroll` reliably,
    and a real wheel does.
  - **An unread row is tinted** `bg-blue-50`. A read one is white.
  - The **Mark as read** tick has an accessible name, from a `title` attribute,
    and goes `disabled` once used. **The trash button has no accessible name at
    all** - no title, no aria-label, no text.
  - **Deleting is not confirmed.** `DELETE /notifications/:id` fires on the
    click and is a hard delete.
  - **Mark all as read is rendered even when there is nothing to mark**, as an
    EMPTY button: `children: unreadCount > 0 ? "Mark all as read" : ""`. So it
    does not disappear, it goes blank.
  - **A Refresh link appears in the header** only while `unreadCount` has grown
    past what it was when the modal opened. Not reachable in a single spec run
    and not documented.
  - The empty state is a tick in a grey circle, **"No notifications"** and
    "You're all caught up! Check back later for new updates."
  - Clicking a row navigates to whatever it is about and marks it read on the
    way.
  - **The badge is Firestore and it drifts.** See "Two defects".
- Dual capture: no.
- Steps:
  1. Look at the bell beside your name in the sidebar. The red number is how

  2. Select the bell to open your notifications.
  3. Scroll the list for older ones.
  4. Select a notification to open whatever it is about.
  5. Select the tick on a row to mark that one read.
  6. Select **Mark all as read** to clear the lot.
  7. Select the bin on a row to delete it.

## The clock is NOT frozen, and that is a decision

`docs/style-guide.md` says to freeze the clock "where the screen shows a date or
a countdown". Every screen this collection photographs shows a **relative** stamp
instead - "2 minutes ago" on a notification row, "7 minutes ago" on a Trending
card - computed as `dayjs(createdAt).fromNow()` against `Date.now()`.

Freezing `Date.now()` does not stabilise those. It corrupts them. Measured with
a 09:00 UTC freeze against a 06:05 UTC seed, every row read **"3 hours ago"**;
and a seed that ran after the frozen instant would render the same rows as "in 3
hours", which is not a state the product has.

So the real clock stands and the stamps read what a reader's would. They differ a
little between runs, which `docs/style-guide.md` allows in as many words. No
article's prose or alt text quotes one.

They are not masked either. Collection 19 masked a comment footer because it
carries a full absolute `03:33 PM • Sep 02, 2026`; a relative "2 minutes ago" is
collection 18's case, where bare stamps were left alone. The whole content of a
Trending card is a short sentence and that stamp, and painting three black bars
across a three-card strip destroys the thing 20.4 describes.

The one absolute date in the collection is the match date, and it comes from the
fixture rather than from a clock.

## Corrections to config/api.md

Appended with today's date, all observed on the wire or in the app:

1. **The notification `type` enum, complete, eighteen values**, printed by the
   API in its own validation error: `MatchLive | FriendAdded | PlayerJoinedTeam
   | PlayerTeamInvitation | MatchInvitation | MatchSummary | MatchReviewRequest
   | LeaderboardAdminAdded | TournamentUpdate | PlayerFollow | TeamFollow |
   LeaderboardComment | CommentReply | CommentLike | ChatMessageSummary |
   PaymentRequested | PaymentReceived | PaymentFailed`. The app's renderer has a
   case for seventeen of them and **none for `PaymentFailed`**.
2. **`GET /notifications` has no counters and the `isRead` filter does not
   work.** The response is a bare array. `?isRead=` answers an empty list for
   every value tried.
3. **The badge comes from Firestore**, `notifications/<firebase uid>`, read with
   an `onSnapshot` listener, fields `{totalCount, unreadCount,
   notificationIds}`. `unreadCount` is a running tally, is not clamped, and
   drifts from the list - the arithmetic is in "Two defects" above.
   `notificationIds` is left holding stale ids by a delete and nothing reads it.
4. **`MatchLive` needs an explicit `POST /matches/:id/status {"status":"Live"}`.**
   The automatic start a passed date causes sends nothing.
5. **A `PlayerFollow` notification is sent once per pair of accounts, for
   ever.** `DELETE /players/:id/follow` then `POST` again answers 200 with the
   **same** `followId` and sends nothing: the record is soft-deleted and
   revived, the way a friend record is. A first-ever follow from an account that
   has never followed that player does notify.
6. **`ONE_FRIEND_PER_TEAM` is wider than `config/api.md` records.** That file
   says "a friend already on one of your teams". On Free,
   `POST /team-players` was refused for a registered player who was on **none**
   of the caller's teams - measured three ways, and again after removing him
   from the only other team he had been added to. Every account is born owning
   two teams, so on Free the refusal is effectively unconditional for a real
   account. Each refusal also soft-deleted the caller's friend record for him,
   which is collection 05's finding holding again.
7. **`PlayerJoinedTeam` goes to the team's Owner AND to its Administrators.**
   That is what lets a Free account receive one at all, given item 6.
8. **`PUT /team-players/:id` re-issues the invitation.** It requires `name` and
   `email` in its body even when all it changes is the role, and sending them
   produces a second `PlayerTeamInvitation`. The reference lists `resendInvite`
   as an option on that call; it evidently does not need to be asked for.
9. `GET /activities` pages with **`page` and `limit`**, not `skip`, and
   `referenceType` is the eight-value enum in 20.4's preconditions.
10. `POST /admins/users` **creates two teams and a leaderboard**, sends the
    "Welcome to Scoryboard, set your password" email, and sets
    `isMarketingOpted: true` and `isEmailVerified: true`. The team names gain a
    date suffix when they clash - `Ollie K FC 0309`.

## Open questions

1. **`ChatMessageSummary` and `MatchReviewRequest` never arrived.** Both are in
   the API's own enum. Two chat messages were sent to Pia and a match was
   finished, and twenty minutes later neither notification existed. Assumed
   scheduled digests. Nothing in this collection documents either, and 20.1
   does not claim to list every kind.
2. **`ONE_FRIEND_PER_TEAM` on Free looks unconditional for a real account**, not
   "one team each" - see correction 6. If that is the intent, the message is
   wrong; if it is not, a Free account cannot build a team out of real accounts
   at all. **Worth a ticket**, and worth re-reading `briefs/05.md` against.
3. **The badge tally can go negative and nothing repairs it.** Correction 3. A
   reader who marks all read and then deletes a few unread notifications ends up
   with a badge that will not show again until they receive as many new ones as
   they deleted. **Worth a ticket.** Nothing in an article mentions it.
4. **`POST /users/verify-email/request` reports `isEmailVerified: false` on a
   verified account**, and sends the code anyway. Harmless and confusing.
   Nothing documented from it.
5. **`KB Notify Cup` is seeded and never photographed.** It exists so that a
   later session can reach a `TournamentCreated` activity and the **Tournament**
   card label cheaply. 20.4 names only the three labels that were seen rendered
   - MATCH, TEAM and PLAYER - and 400 rows of the real feed hold no
   `leaderboard`, `user`, `friend` or `comment` activity at all, so two of the
   five words the bundle can print may never occur in practice. Worth checking
   again when the platform has more data on it.
6. **The Refresh link in the notification modal header** appears only while the
   Firestore tally has grown past what it was when the modal opened. Not
   reachable in one spec run, so not documented.
7. **`notificationIds` on the Firestore document** holds a stale list after any
   delete. Nothing in the app reads it. Assumed vestigial.
