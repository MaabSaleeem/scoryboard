Persona default: player   Feature flag: none

Everything this collection photographs is a **derived number**: match insights, a
side-by-side team comparison, two grids of statistic tiles, a per-player table, a
per-leaderboard table. None of it exists until matches have actually been played,
and a played match cannot be undone. That single fact shapes the whole plan - the
fixture is a small league that has really run, and no spec here changes a number.

### Why 11.1 is retitled

The map calls it *"Match facts, insights, form and head-to-head"*. Two of those
four things are not in the product.

- **There is no form guide.** No W/D/L strip anywhere on a match, a team or a
  player. The nearest thing is the Insights panel, whose five lines are each
  about "its last 5 games" - so the form information exists, as sentences, and
  the article points the reader at it.
- **There is no head-to-head record.** The panel that looks like one, *Statistics
  so far*, is each team's **whole** record in the leaderboard, shown side by side.
  This was settled by observation rather than reasoning - see
  [Fixtures](#fixtures) and open question 1.

The new title uses the app's own two words, *Insights* and *Statistics so far*,
so a reader who is looking at the screen recognises it. The article still answers
the head-to-head question, by saying plainly that the panel is not one.

### API observed, and what config/api.md gets wrong

All appended to `config/api.md` marked `(observed in app, 2026-08-31)`.

1. **`GET /matches/:id/facts` is not admin-only.** `config/api.md` lists it under
   the admin key. The app calls it with the signed-in user's bearer token, on
   every match page, alongside `GET /matches/:id/facts-stats`. Both answer 200
   for a player who is in the lineup.
2. **`facts` answers `{homeTeam: [{text, teamName}], awayTeam: [...]}`** - five
   sentences per team, already written out. The app renders the strings as it gets
   them, which is why one of them is "It's been 0 days since ...".
3. **`facts-stats` carries no `conceded` figure and no draw count.** Its per-team
   `stats` block is `{totalWins, totalLosses, totalMatches, goalScored, winStreak,
   cleanSheets, totalYellowCards, totalRedCards, teamMembers, isRanked, rank}`.
   So the panel's **Goals conceded per match is always a dash**, and *Drawn* is
   worked out in the browser as matches minus wins minus losses.
4. **Empty facts mean no leaderboard, and nothing else.** A match with no
   `leaderboardId` answers `facts` with two empty arrays and `facts-stats` with no
   team stats. Isolated against the match tag, which was the other candidate: a
   `friendly` that IS in a leaderboard has all five insights, and a `league` that
   is not has none.
5. **`statsCalculatedAt` is on the match object**, next to `startedAt` and
   `finishedAt`, along with `queueName`. On this collection's four matches it
   landed **1.6, 5.7, 6.4 and 6.8 seconds** after `finishedAt`.
6. **A future-dated match is born `Incomplete`, not `Scheduled`.**
7. **`POST /matches` accepts a short or an empty lineup.** A one-player
   `players[]` and an empty one were both accepted on a 5 VS 5 match.
8. **A clean sheet is not simply conceding nothing.** The 0-0 draw scored **no**
   clean sheet for either team; the 4-0 win scored one. Both read off
   `GET /teams/:id/stats`.
9. **A player's statistics count matches they were in the lineup for.** Rovers
   have played four; Pia, left out of one, reads three.
10. **`GET /players/:id/matches` does not agree with that.** Pia's Matches list on
    Home shows the 4-0 she was not in - it is her teams' fixtures. So the list and
    the tile count different things, which is worth its own line in 11.2.

## Open questions

1. **Is *Statistics so far* ever a head-to-head record?** Answered no, by
   observation: on the Rovers v City page it shows Rovers 4 matches to City's 3,
   and Rovers' biggest win is 4-0 against a third team. What was **not** tested is
   whether a team in two leaderboards gets one panel per leaderboard or a merged
   one - the panel names one leaderboard at its head, so presumably the match's
   own. Assumed: the numbers are the team's record in the match's leaderboard.
2. **How is the in-team Rank ordered?** Not established. Goals outrank matches
   played (Pia, 3 matches, is above Otto, 4), but Rory is above Pia on identical
   goals and assists, and Otto and Sam have identical rows and different ranks.
   The article describes the column and does not claim a formula.
3. **VIEW KEY on the Facts card points at the wrong legend.** It scrolls to the
   KEYS accordion, which explains lineup and feed icons and says nothing about
   the two panels or their colours. Assumed to be a wiring mistake; the article
   names the button and does not promise it explains the Facts tab.
4. **Why does a 0-0 draw score no clean sheet?** Observed twice, on both teams.
   The 4-0 win scored one. Assumed the rule is "won without conceding", which is
   what the article says - carefully, as an observation about what the number
   does, not as a documented rule.
5. **Goals conceded per match is always a dash.** The endpoint carries no
   conceded figure, so this is not a fixture problem and no data will fix it. The
   article tells the reader where to find the figure instead (the team's
   Goals / Conceded tile). Worth a ticket.
6. **"It's been 0 days since KB 11 Rovers's last game"** does not agree with the
   match dates - the last match is dated 20 August and the app was read on 31
   August. It looks to be counting from when the match row was created rather
   than from the match date, which means it will read differently on every re-run
   of the spec. Not masked: it is one of the five lines the article is about, and
   masking it would leave a black bar in the middle of the subject.
7. **A followed-up figure nobody asked for:** `POST /matches` accepts an empty
   lineup on a 5 VS 5 match. Not documented in any article - it belongs to
   collection 09 if it belongs anywhere - but it is in `config/api.md` now.

## Changed during step 2

Four things, all mechanical, none of them a change to what an article says.

1. **11.1's first capture is a viewport shot, not a clip of the tab strip.** Both
   of the strip's `[role="tablist"]` containers have **zero height** - one is
   `h-0 p-0` - and the tabs overflow them, so `onScreen()` discarded both and
   `.first()` fell through to the **Pay** tablist in the payment section, 3,200
   pixels down the page. That is what the first run came back with. The viewport
   shot is also the better picture: it shows the banner, the tab strip and the
   Match Details card, which orients a reader who has just opened a match. The
   sidebar name and the notification badge are masked because the sidebar is now
   in frame. Recorded in `matchTab()` in `lib/kb.ts`.
2. **All three of 11.2's captures gained `clipPad: 12`.** The MATCHES tile is in
   the grid's top-left corner and the annotated row is the full width of the
   table, so in both cases the red rectangle was drawn outside the clip and came
   back with two of its four edges missing.
3. **11.2's third capture gained an annotation** the brief did not plan: the
   persona's row. It is the article's whole argument - three matches where every
   other row reads four - and the shot was doing nothing to point at it.
4. **11.1's Facts card is clipped to the card, not the section wrapper.**
   `#facts` is a bare wrapper whose box starts a few pixels above the white card,
   and those pixels are the banner photo - the first run had a dark seam across
   the top edge. `matchSectionCard()` in `lib/kb.ts` takes the card.

Two selector faults were fixed in the same pass and are worth knowing for
collections 09 and 10, which own this screen:

- **`waitUntil: 'networkidle'` never resolves on a match page.** The presence
  connection behind the feed's ONLINE badge keeps the network busy, so every
  navigation times out at 30 seconds. `openMatch()` waits for content instead.
- **The MATCH ENDED pill is `Match Ended` in the DOM.** Upper-cased by CSS, like
  GROUP A in 14 and DELETE ACCOUNT in 01.

Nothing else differed. Nine screenshots, as planned, in the three articles the
map lists.
